Kiwi Extinction

How you can help prevent kiwis from going extinct

Species Extinction In New Zealand

Species extinction has been happening in New Zealand since humans first arrived. Over 75 native New Zealand animals have gone
extinct, this makes New Zealand have one of the highest rates of native species extinction in the world. Of all of the species,
some of the most important ones include the Huia birds, Moa, Haast's Eagle, Lyalls' Wren and Laughing Owls. Many of the extinct
species include birds, which are very important to New Zealands culture as their feathers are often used to make korowais. These
cloaks are a large part of Maori culture, representing prestige, honour, leadership and identity. If New Zealand continues not
taking care of their natives animals, it is very likly that one day there will be no natives animals left in New Zealand. There
is about 80,000 native species(including plants and animals) left in New Zealand, and 4,000 of them are either threatened or
endangered. 50% of New Zealands bird species have gone extinct since human arrival.

Extinction Of Kiwis

Kiwis are the second most endangered species in the world with only around 68,000 left. There population has been decreasing
by about 2 precent every year which is roughly 20 kiwis per week.At this rate, kiwis will be extinct in the next 50 years.
Kiwis are an extremly valuble to New Zealand as they are a large part of our cultural identity and seen by Maori as a taonga.
There are many reasons why kiwis population is declining quickly such as habitat loss caused by humans making space for
building and farm land, being attacked by stoats, ferrets, cats and dogs. They are also more vulnerable to crushing injurys,
such as dog bites, as they have under developed wings, chest muscles and no breastbone. Many native flightless birds have
gone extinct in New Zealand, with there being only around 16 species left, 5 of them being kiwi species all of them being
endangered. A majority of the other native flightless birds are at risk of going extinct as well, the Kakapo is most at
risk as there are only around 235 birds left. Many native species are at risk, which is why helping at least one species
will make a big difference.
