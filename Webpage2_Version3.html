Kaitiakitanga

How you can be a kaitiaki

What is Kaitiakitanga?

Kaitiakitanga is a Maori word that means guardianship and protection. It is derived from the word
'tiaki' which means to preserve or protect. It is the responsibility New Zealand has to protect and
nurture the land, sky, sea and resourses for the future generations.This stems from the belief that
humans are linked to the natural world, so we are responsible for maintaining natural balance. It is
a mutual relationship between the land taking care of us and us taking care of the land. Kaitiakitanga
is important as it protects the natural world such as our land, rivers and native animals, rather the
allowing them to be exploited and destroyed. It ensures that natural resources are passed down to future
generations in a healthy state, allowing guardianship to be continued. It also reinforced the connection
between Maori and the environment, strengthening cultural identity, and restoring/protecting natural
sites. Us taking care of the environment means that the environment will take care of us in return. This
has been a very important part of Maori culture for many years, it is very important that we keep up this
tradition and ideal as it is beneficial for everyone in NZ. Many other countrys follow similar ideas of
kaitiakitanga, such as Canada, America, and Australia, so everyone is collectively protecting the earths
enviroment in their own ways. Kaitiakitanga can be about protecting sacred taonga like family heirlooms,
genealogy and stories. This ideal is recognised in official New Zealand laws, claiming developers must
take kaitiakitanga into account when making decisions about the land and water.

How To Be A Kaitiaki For Kiwis

There are many ways that you can help protect kiwis as a part of kaitiakitanga. Dogs are a common
predator to kiwis, so keep them contained on your property and leashed during walks near forests.
If you go hunting with your dog, make sure they are trained with a kiwi adversion permit to prevent them
from attacking kiwis. Keeping cats inside will also help kiwis, as cats are yet another common predator.
Reporting stray dogs near forests also prevent kiwis being attacked, as people will be able to bring the
dog to the spca or elsewhere to ensure the kiwis are safe. If driving near kiwi habitat, which is indicated
with a yellow kiwi sign on the road, make sure you are driving carefully and looking out for kiwis. If you
spot an injured kiwi, you should take them to the vet as it will be free of charge (discuss with DOC). You
can transport the kiwi in a ventilated box, but make sure to store it in a cool and secure place, avoiding
loud noises. If you find a dead kiwi, you should take a photo of where you found it and report it. Putting
it into a bag and keeping in the fridge will give the DOC a chance to come and see it to gather important
information as to what killed it, allowing other kiwis to be protected from the same issue. These steps
may seem quite simple and like they may not make that big of an impact on the kiwi population, however
they do. This allows kiwis to have a safe habitat to live in and breed at. It also allows the wildlife
professionals that take care of New Zealand species and animals to get a better understanding on what is
threatening the kiwi population so they can try prevent it to keep them safe. If you want to help more to
protect kiwis, there are many help organisations and charities in New Zealand that focus on looking after
and growing the kiwi pouplation. These organisations have had great progess with their efforts to help
kiwis, so joining them or at least helping by donating allows them to further protect and help kiwis.

Help Organisations

Department Of Conservation
DOC is a good website to check out if you wish to learn more information about how to keep kiwis, other animals, and the rest of the environment
in New Zealand safe and protected. They are a public service department of New Zealand responsible for protecting the countries natural and historical
heritage. This is especially important to check out if you go hunting, fishing, or camping as it provides useful tips on how to be prepared and how
you can have fun and take care of the enviroment at the same time. You can also volunteer to help with many activitys and take free online courses
to learn about how to take care of the enviroment yourself. Some of these courses include animal ecology, bird identification and wildlife health.

Save The Kiwi
This organisation work alongside many iwi, conservation groups and communities to reverse the decline of kiwi population. They do this by creating
more habitats for kiwis and bringing awareness to their endangerment and importance to New Zealand. They also run a kiwi breeding and repopulation
program to help further increase the population of them, so far they have hatched 454 eggs at the Gallagher Kiwi Burrow since it opened in 2019.
They also train dogs in the official Kiwi Avoidance Training programme, which lowers the rate of kiwis getting attacked by dogs.This can be a good
organisation to join as they work with communities and you are able to help in simple ways such as donating, participating in fundrasiers, reporting
kiwis, and buying items from their store.

Project Kiwi
Project Kiwi has been managing kiwi conservation since 1996, making them the oldest communtiy-led kiwi conservation project in the world. The Trust
began when a local Kūaotunu community wanted to reduce the threat of predators to kiwis, so begun to kill their predators. They use methods such as
pest and predator management to help protect and restore kiwi population. They have also invested in habitat and biodiversity inventory to provide more
space for kiwi habitat. When they first started, the local kiwi population was around 535 birds, but in 2010 there was 600 birds, showing their efforts
were making progress. They have also released 225 kiwis into the wild from their Operation Nest Egg programme. There are many ways to support their
organisation, such as buying them predator traps,donating, and even partnering with them if you are part of an enviromental organisation yourself.
