Kaitiakitanga

How you can be a kaitiaki

What is Kaitiakitanga?

Kaitiakitanga is a Maori word that means guardianship and protection. It is the responsibility
New Zealand has to protect and nurture the land, sky, sea and resourses for the future generations
This steams from the belief that humans are linked to the natural world, so we are responsible
for maintaining natural balance. It is a mutual relationship between the land taking care of us
and us taking care of the land. Kaitiakitanga is important as it protects the natural world such as
our land, rivers and native species, rather then allowing them to be exploited and destroyed. It
ensures that resources are passed down to future generations in a healthy state, allowing gaurdianship
to be continued. It also reinforced the connection between Maori and the enviroment, strengthening
cultural identity, and restoring/protecting natural sites. Us taking care of the enviroment means that
the enviroment will take care of us in return.

How To Be A Kaitiaki For Kiwis

There are many ways that you can help protect kiwis as a part of kaitiakitanga. Dogs are a common
predator to kiwis, so keeping them contained on your property and leashed during walks near forests.
If you go hunting with you dog, make sure they are trained with a kiwi adversion permit to prevent them
from attacking kiwis. Keeping cats inside will also help kiwis, as cats are yet another common predator.
Reporting stray dogs near forests also prevent kiwis being attacked, as people will be able to bring the
dog to the spca or elsewhere to ensure the kiwis are safe. If driving near kiwi habitat, which is indicated
with a yellow kiwi sign on the road, make sure you are driving carefully and looking out for kiwis. If you
spot an injured kiwi, you should take them to the vet as it will be free of charge (discuss with DOC). You
can transport the kiwi in a ventilated box, but make sure to store it in a cool and secure place, avoiding
loud noises. If you find a dead kiwi, you should take a photo of where you found it and report it. Putting
it into a bag and keeping in the fridge will give the DOC important information as to what killed it, allowing
kiwis to be protected from the same issue.
