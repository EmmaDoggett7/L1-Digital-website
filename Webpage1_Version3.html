Kiwi Extinction

How you can help prevent kiwis from going extinct

Extinction In New Zealand

Species extinction has been happening in New Zealand since humans first arrived.
Over 75 native New Zealand animals have gone extinct, this makes New Zealand have
one of the highest rates of native animal extinction in the world. Out of all all of the
species, some of the most important ones include the Huia birds, Moa, Haast's Eagle,
Lyalls' Wren and Laughing Owls. Many of the extinct species include birds, which are
very important to New Zealand's culture as their feathers are often used to make korowai.
These cloaks are a large part of Maori culture, representing prestige, honour,
leadership and identity. If New Zealand continues not taking care of their natives
animals, it is very likely that one day there will be no natives animals left in New
Zealand. There is about 80,000 native species(including plants and animals) left in
New Zealand, and 4,000 of them are either threatened or endangered. 50% of New Zealand's
bird species have gone extinct since human arrival. The main reasons they have gone
extinct are due to predators, vulnerablity due to many being flightless, habitat loss
and being hunted by humans. Some of these reasons still remain the most common way New
Zealand species, particularly birds, are becoming endangered and soon to add to the already
large number of native species that have gone extinct. There are many organisations and
charities that are trying to protect our native species and enviroment, many of them have
made great progress, but they still need people to become aware of these issues so they can
do their part to protect New Zealand species as well.

Extinction Of Kiwis

Kiwis are the second most endangered species in the world with only around 68,000
left. This is a very low number, considering there used to be an estimate of 12 million
kiwis in New Zealand many years ago, and one-hundred-thousand in 2000. There population has
been decreasing by about two percent every year which is roughly 20 kiwis per week. At this
rate, kiwis will be extinct in the next 50 years. Rowi, a species of kiwi is the rarest of
the five species, with only 450 left. Kiwis are an extremly valuble to New Zealandas they
are a large part of our cultural identity and seen by Maori as a taonga. There are many
reasons why the kiwi birds population is declining, such as habitat loss caused by humans
making space for building and farm land, being attacked by stoats, ferrets, cats and dogs.
They are also more vulnerable to crushing injurys, such as dog bites, as they have under
developed wings, chest muscles and no breastbone. Many native flightless birds have gone
extinct in New Zealand, with there being only around 16 species left, five of them being kiwi
species all of them being endangered. A majority of the other native flightless birds are at
risk of going extinct as well, which is why helping at least one species will make a big
difference, expecially because kiws are a large part of NZ's culture. Many organisations
have made great progress in growing the kiwi population. Some of the most successful incude
Save The Kiwi, Capital Kiwi Project, and Santuary Mountain Maungatautari. With organisations
like these, the local kiwi populations rise by 2%, the same ammount it is declining by in
areas with no kiwi protection. The DOC has a goal to try reach one-hundred-thousand kiwi
popuplation by 2030. This can only be possibe with breeding programs, translocations, and
pest control.
